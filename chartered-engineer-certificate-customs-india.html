What Is a Chartered Engineer Certificate for Customs?

A Chartered Engineer Certificate for Customs is a technical certificate prepared by a suitably qualified engineer for a specific purpose related to imported goods, machinery or industrial assets.

Depending on the case, the assessment may cover equipment specifications, technical details, machinery condition, valuation or installation. The scope and format of the certificate depend on the requirements of the relevant authority or applicable scheme.

Importers may encounter Chartered Engineer certification requirements in connection with:

  • Technical assessment of imported machinery via Used Machinery Valuation (explore our Chennai Importers Guide)
  • Verification of machinery specifications and descriptions
  • Used machinery inspection and valuation
  • Certification related to eligible import authorisations
  • Installation of capital goods under EPCG & Advance Authorisation and Installation / Nexus certification
  • Documentation for SEZ units with SEZ certification, factory stability certificates, or metal scrap radiation testing

A Chartered Engineer Certificate is not mandatory for every import into India. Businesses should confirm the specific requirement before arranging an inspection.

Why Is a Chartered Engineer Certificate Required?

The purpose of a Chartered Engineer Certificate varies according to the transaction and the applicable rules. It may support technical information or provide an independent engineering assessment when such certification is required.

1. Technical Verification of Imported Machinery

Industrial machinery can involve complex specifications, components, accessories and operating capacities. A technical assessment can help document relevant details such as equipment type, model, capacity, manufacturer and technical configuration.

2. Used Machinery Valuation

Used machinery may require an assessment of its age, condition, specifications, usage and other relevant factors when an accredited used machinery survey and valuation report is requested. For detailed evaluation criteria, explore our guide on machinery valuation factors.

3. EPCG Related Certification

Under the Export Promotion Capital Goods (EPCG) scheme, specific certification requirements may apply to eligible capital goods. Depending on the purpose, these may include an Installation Certificate confirming installation or an EPCG & Advance Authorisation Certificate supporting the technical relationship between capital goods and the relevant export product or activity. For procedural step-by-step guidance, refer to our EPCG installation certificate guide.

The required certificate, format and submission procedure depend on the applicable scheme provisions and the circumstances of the case.

4. Support for Import Documentation

A technical certificate may form part of the supporting documentation for a particular import related process. The certificate should address the specific requirements of the assignment rather than rely on a generic format.

Documents Required for a Chartered Engineer Certificate

The required documents depend on the type of certificate, the equipment being assessed and the purpose of the assignment. The following checklist can help businesses prepare for an initial review.

Commonly Required Documents

  • Commercial invoice or pro forma invoice, where applicable
  • Packing list and equipment details
  • Bill of Entry, if available
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, where relevant
  • Manufacturer's technical specifications and product catalogue
  • Equipment make, model, serial number and year of manufacture
  • Machinery photographs and nameplate details
  • Purchase order or relevant commercial documents
  • Previous inspection or valuation reports, if available

Additional Documents for Specific Requirements

For used machinery valuation:

  • Equipment age and usage history
  • Available maintenance and repair records
  • Operating condition details
  • Purchase price or other relevant transaction information
  • Supporting evidence of equipment specifications and condition

For EPCG installation certification:

  • EPCG authorisation details
  • Relevant Bills of Entry
  • Machinery identification and quantity details
  • Installation address and supporting equipment details
  • Evidence of machinery installation
  • Any prescribed certificate format or supporting documents required for the applicable procedure

This is a general preparation checklist. The final document requirements should be confirmed for the specific case.

Process for Obtaining a Chartered Engineer Certificate for Customs

The process depends on the purpose of certification and the requirements communicated by the relevant authority. A typical workflow includes the following steps.

Step 1: Identify the Certification Requirement

Determine why the certificate is required. The assignment may relate to used machinery valuation, technical verification, an EPCG Nexus Certificate or an Installation Certificate. Confirm the applicable certificate format and whether a physical inspection is required.

Step 2: Submit the Supporting Documents

Provide the available commercial documents, machinery specifications, import details and any prescribed certificate format. The engineer can review the information and identify additional details needed for the assignment.

Step 3: Arrange the Technical Inspection

Where required, a site visit or physical inspection may be arranged to examine the machinery or equipment.

Depending on the assignment, the inspection may cover:

  • Equipment identification and specifications
  • Manufacturer, model and serial number
  • Physical condition and visible damage
  • Machinery age and usage, where relevant
  • Installation status, where applicable
  • Technical details required for the specified certificate

The inspection scope should be agreed upon before the assessment begins.

Step 4: Conduct the Technical Assessment

The engineer reviews the available documentation and inspection findings against the purpose of the assignment. If valuation is required, the report should identify the valuation basis, assumptions and relevant limitations.

Step 5: Prepare the Certificate or Report

Following the assessment, the certificate or technical report is prepared in the applicable format and based on the verified information. Prescribed declarations and supporting details should be included where required.

Step 6: Submit the Certificate to the Relevant Authority

The importer or authorisation holder submits the certificate through the applicable procedure. Additional documents or clarifications may be requested by the relevant authority.

Obtaining a Chartered Engineer Certificate does not automatically guarantee Customs clearance or approval of a particular claim.

Chartered Engineer Certificate for EPCG: What Importers Should Know

The Export Promotion Capital Goods scheme is intended to facilitate the import of eligible capital goods subject to applicable conditions.

Two types of certification businesses may encounter are:

Nexus Certificate: A technical certificate supporting the relationship between capital goods and the relevant export product or activity for an applicable EPCG requirement.

Installation Certificate: A certificate confirming the installation of relevant capital goods at the authorised premises or another permitted location, as applicable.

These certificates serve different purposes and should not be treated as interchangeable. Businesses should verify the current scheme provisions, prescribed formats, submission requirements and applicable deadlines before proceeding.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Preparing the correct documentation before arranging an inspection can help reduce avoidable delays.

  • Using an incorrect certificate format: Confirm the applicable format before commissioning the certificate.
  • Providing incomplete machinery details: Ensure equipment descriptions, quantities, models and serial numbers are accurate.
  • Ignoring document discrepancies: Check that invoices, Bills of Entry, authorisation details and machinery records are consistent.
  • Delaying installation certification: Track the applicable deadline under the relevant scheme and authorisation.
  • Confusing valuation with certification: A machinery valuation report, technical certificate and Installation Certificate may serve different purposes.
  • Assuming certification guarantees approval: The relevant authority determines whether the submitted documents meet its requirements.
