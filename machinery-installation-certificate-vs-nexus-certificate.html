Introduction

Businesses importing industrial machinery under the Export Promotion Capital Goods (EPCG) Scheme may encounter different engineering certification requirements during the import and post-import stages.

Two important certificates associated with EPCG are the Machinery Installation Certificate and the Nexus Certificate. Although both involve technical assessment by an independent Chartered Engineer, they serve different purposes and are generally required at completely different stages of the EPCG lifecycle.

Understanding the difference between these certificates helps importers prepare the appropriate technical documents, avoid confusion during authorization procedures, avoid costly penalties, and meet applicable Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) compliance guidelines.

What Is a Machinery Installation Certificate?

A Machinery Installation Certificate is a technical document confirming that imported capital goods have been installed at the factory or declared premises of the EPCG authorization holder or its supporting manufacturer, as applicable.

Under the EPCG Scheme, the installation certificate provides official evidentiary proof to the DGFT and Customs authorities that the imported machinery or capital goods have arrived and are erected at the declared manufacturing premises.

The certificate may be obtained from the jurisdictional Customs authority or an independent Chartered Engineer, at the option of the authorization holder, in accordance with the applicable Handbook of Procedures (HBP).

Purpose of a Machinery Installation Certificate

The certificate helps establish:

  • Whether the imported machinery has been physically installed at the declared premises.
  • Whether the equipment corresponds with the relevant import documentation (Bill of Entry, commercial invoices, and packing lists).
  • The identification, make, model, capacity, and technical details of the machinery.
  • The installation status and operational positioning of the capital goods in the production cycle.
  • Relevant observations and findings from the physical inspection conducted on-site.

It is an indispensable document in the post-import compliance process under EPCG. For a detailed breakdown of the installation verification process, explore our complete guide on EPCG installation certificate services and requirements.

What Is a Nexus Certificate?

A Nexus Certificate is a technical certification used to establish the direct technological relationship between the capital goods proposed for import and the export products intended to be manufactured or produced using those goods under the EPCG Scheme.

The certificate demonstrates that the proposed machinery has a relevant technical connection with the export product or manufacturing activity, ensuring that concessional import duty benefits are applied strictly to eligible production assets.

Under Paragraph 5.03 of the Handbook of Procedures, the Regional Authority (RA) considers a Nexus Certificate from an independent Chartered Engineer, submitted in Appendix 5A, when evaluating and issuing an EPCG authorization.

Purpose of a Nexus Certificate

A Nexus Certificate helps establish:

  • The technical relationship between the proposed machinery and the targeted export products.
  • The specific role and intended use of the capital goods in the overall manufacturing process.
  • The relevance of machinery specifications, precision ratings, and capacity to the production activity.
  • The technical justification demonstrating why the proposed equipment is essential for export manufacturing.
  • Reasonable wastage anticipated during fabrication, processing, or testing, where applicable.

In accordance with DGFT guidelines, the Chartered Engineer must issue the certificate only within their registered area of professional engineering competence.

Machinery Installation Certificate vs. Nexus Certificate: Key Differences

Although both certificates involve engineering evaluation by a qualified professional, their legal scope, statutory timing, and inspection procedures are fundamentally distinct:

Feature Machinery Installation Certificate Nexus Certificate
Main Purpose Confirms physical installation of capital goods at declared factory Establishes technical connection between machinery and export products
EPCG Stage After import and physical installation Before import, at the authorization application stage
Main Focus Physical presence, machine serial numbers, and installation status Technical relevance of machinery specifications to export production
Inspection Type Physical on-site inspection of installed machinery and foundation Technical evaluation of machinery specs and manufacturing flow
Issuing Professional Jurisdictional Customs authority or independent Chartered Engineer Independent Chartered Engineer (Appendix 5A)
HBP Regulatory Provision Handbook of Procedures Paragraph 5.04 Handbook of Procedures Paragraph 5.03
Statutory Submission Submitted to Regional Authority (RA) and Customs within 3 years Submitted to DGFT Regional Authority along with EPCG application

Important Compliance Warning: These certificates are not interchangeable. A Nexus Certificate does not prove that machinery has been installed, and an Installation Certificate does not establish the technical nexus between machinery and export products. Submitting the wrong certificate can result in severe application delays or customs queries.

When Is a Nexus Certificate Required?

A Nexus Certificate is required at the authorization application stage before the capital goods are shipped or cleared through customs.

The applicant provides the certificate as part of the formal application documentation submitted on the DGFT portal. It assists the Regional Authority in verifying whether the proposed capital goods have an authentic technical nexus with the applicant's declared export products.

Practical Industry Example: Consider a precision automotive components manufacturer in Chennai or Bangalore intending to export multi-axis transmission gears. To manufacture these parts to international tolerances, the enterprise proposes importing a 5-axis CNC machining center and coordinate measuring machine (CMM) under EPCG concessional duty.

A Chartered Engineer assesses the technical specifications, cutting tooling, production tolerances, and process flowsheets to certify that the CNC equipment directly creates the export components. The certificate is drawn up in Appendix 5A format based on actual production requirements and DGFT parameters.

When Is a Machinery Installation Certificate Required?

A Machinery Installation Certificate is required after the capital goods have been imported and erected at the factory premises specified in the EPCG authorization.

The certificate confirms the physical installation status of the capital goods and provides verified documentary evidence required for post-import compliance. The Chartered Engineer's on-site verification typically involves:

  • Physical inspection of the machinery at the factory floor.
  • Cross-verification of manufacturer, brand, model, and year of manufacture.
  • Recording exact machinery serial numbers from factory nameplates.
  • Reviewing commercial invoices, packing lists, and Bill of Entry documents.
  • Confirming that the installation location matches the premises registered in the EPCG authorization.
  • Documenting photographic evidence showing the machine erected, wired, and connected to utilities.

Documents Required for Both Certificates

The documentation requirements vary depending on whether the assignment involves establishing pre-import technical nexus or verifying post-import physical installation:

Documents for a Nexus Certificate

  • Company profile and manufacturing unit details
  • EPCG online application draft
  • Itemized list of proposed capital goods
  • Machinery technical specifications & OEM brochures
  • HS Code classifications for machinery and export products
  • Detailed manufacturing process flow description
  • Production capacity and cycle time calculations
  • Technical justification explaining export necessity
  • Anticipated fabrication wastage percentages

Documents for an Installation Certificate

  • Copy of the issued EPCG Authorisation
  • Customs Out of Charge Bill of Entry
  • Commercial invoices and overseas supplier packing lists
  • Machinery make, model, and serial number records
  • Factory registration or industrial license copy
  • Address proof of the declared installation premises
  • High-resolution photographs of installed machinery
  • Supporting electrical connection or test run logs

EPCG Compliance: Important Timeline for Installation Certificates

Importers must pay meticulous attention to statutory submission deadlines to maintain their EPCG licenses in good standing:

DGFT Public Notice No. 15/2024-25 Timeline Guidelines

Under Paragraph 5.04 of the current EPCG Handbook of Procedures, the authorization holder is required to submit the Installation Certificate to the concerned Regional Authority within three years from the date of completion of import.

The Regional Authority may grant an extension up to the validity of the export obligation period, subject to payment of a composition fee of ₹10,000 per year. Where an independent Chartered Engineer issues the certificate, a copy must also be forwarded to the jurisdictional Customs Authority for intimation and record.

To avoid recurring composition fees or procedural non-compliance notices, businesses should arrange physical inspections promptly after equipment erection.

Common Mistakes Importers Should Avoid

Handling EPCG documentation requires rigorous precision. Importers frequently encounter avoidable roadblocks due to the following common mistakes:

1. Confusing the Two Certificates

Treating a Nexus Certificate and an Installation Certificate as interchangeable leads to immediate rejection by the DGFT portal or Customs audit teams.

2. Discrepancies in Machinery Details

Mismatches between the machine model number declared on the Bill of Entry and the physical serial plate on the machine can trigger customs investigations.

3. Incomplete Technical Descriptions

Omitting OEM brochures, process diagrams, or production metrics prevents the Chartered Engineer from validating the engineering nexus.

4. Installing Machinery at an Undeclared Location

Erecting equipment at a subsidiary branch or warehouse without prior DGFT endorsement is a violation of EPCG authorization conditions.

5. Delayed Installation Certificate Submission

Missing the 3-year deadline leads to compounding composition fees and blocks future advance authorization license renewals. Learn how to prevent these pitfalls in our guide on EPCG scheme documentation mistakes importers should avoid.

6. Inadequate Technical Justification

Failing to establish how auxiliary machinery (e.g., chillers, air compressors, testing rigs) directly contributes to export production can jeopardize authorization approval.

Conclusion

Machinery Installation Certificates and Nexus Certificates fulfill distinct statutory mandates throughout the EPCG compliance journey. The Nexus Certificate validates technological relevance during the initial authorization application, while the Installation Certificate certifies physical deployment and readiness on the factory floor.

Understanding these procedural differences ensures that Indian importers structure their technical filings correctly, minimize regulatory bottlenecks, and safeguard their export benefits.
