What Is an EPCG Installation Certificate?

An EPCG Installation Certificate is a document confirming that capital goods imported under an EPCG authorisation have been installed at the specified factory or premises of the authorisation holder or supporting manufacturer.

Looking for Nexus vs Installation comparison? Explore our comprehensive guide on Machinery Installation Certificate vs. Nexus Certificate Differences under the EPCG scheme.

The certificate may be obtained from the jurisdictional Customs authority or an independent Chartered Engineer, subject to the applicable DGFT provisions.

It provides documentary evidence of machinery installation and supports the authorisation holder's EPCG compliance requirements.

The certificate generally records details such as:

  • EPCG authorisation number
  • Import and machinery details
  • Manufacturer and model information
  • Machinery serial number
  • Installation location
  • Physical inspection findings
  • Installation confirmation
  • Relevant supporting documents

Why Is an EPCG Installation Certificate Required?

The EPCG Scheme is intended to facilitate the import of capital goods for producing goods or providing services, subject to applicable export-obligation conditions.

An installation certificate helps establish that the imported capital goods have been installed at the authorised premises.

Businesses may require this certificate for:

  • EPCG authorisation compliance: Meeting statutory requirements stipulated by DGFT and Indian Customs.
  • DGFT documentation and submission: Uploading official proof on the DGFT portal.
  • Verification of imported capital goods: Validating that the imported machines match the declared Bill of Entry specifications.
  • Machinery installation confirmation: Ensuring equipment is functional at the declared factory address.
  • Export obligation-related documentation: Laying the necessary foundation for subsequent export obligation discharge and redemption.
  • Supporting records for import and machinery compliance: Providing an independent technical audit trail.

The certificate should be prepared based on the actual machinery, inspection findings, and applicable requirements.

For businesses seeking EPCG certification services and EPCG inspection Chennai, the process generally involves the following structured steps:

Step 1: Review EPCG Authorisation Details

The first step is to review the EPCG authorisation and related import documents. Important details include the authorisation number, imported capital goods, machinery description, quantity, and relevant conditions. This helps establish whether the machinery being inspected corresponds with the authorisation and import records.

Step 2: Collect the Required Documents

The business should arrange the available import, machinery, and installation-related documents before the inspection. These records help verify the identity of the equipment and its installation details.

Step 3: Physical Inspection of Machinery

A Chartered Engineer visits the factory or installation premises in Chennai or the relevant location to inspect the imported capital goods. The inspection covers:

  • Machinery identification and serial numbers
  • Manufacturer and model details
  • Physical presence and foundation mounting of the equipment
  • Installation and positioning in the production flow
  • Available technical specifications
  • Machinery condition and visible operating arrangements
  • Supporting documents and operational records

Step 4: Verification of Installation Details

The inspection findings are reviewed against the EPCG authorisation and supporting documents. Any differences in machinery descriptions, quantities, identification details, or installation locations should be examined and clarified before certification.

Step 5: Preparation of the Installation Certificate

After reviewing the available documents and inspection findings, the Chartered Engineer prepares the installation certificate in the applicable format and scope. The certificate accurately reflects the machinery inspected, nameplate details, and verified installation status.

Step 6: Submission Through the DGFT Portal

The authorisation holder is responsible for submitting the installation certificate through the applicable DGFT online facility. The DGFT EPCG user manual describes the online workflow for uploading an installation certificate, including authorisation details, attachments, declarations, digital signing, and submission. Where an independent Chartered Engineer's certificate is used, a copy is also required to be sent to the jurisdictional Customs authority for intimation or record, as applicable.

Documents Required for EPCG Installation Certificate

The exact document list may vary depending on the EPCG authorisation, machinery type, import details, and inspection requirements. Commonly requested documents include:

Document Purpose
EPCG authorisation copy Verifies authorisation details, duty exemption scope, and conditions
Bill of Entry Provides Customs import information, clearance dates, and duty assessment
Commercial invoice Supports machinery purchase details, value, and supplier specifications
Packing list Helps verify imported equipment packages, items, and dimensions
Bill of Lading or Airway Bill Supports shipment details, freight mode, and origin details
Machinery technical specifications Identifies equipment capabilities, power ratings, and output ratings
Manufacturer details Confirms brand, country of manufacture, and model series
Machinery serial number details Supports equipment identification against nameplates and documents
Installation photographs Provides visual installation evidence, serial plate shots, and site context
Factory address and details Confirms installation location as declared in the EPCG authorisation
Import and machinery records Supports comprehensive document verification and internal compliance

Additional documents may be requested depending on the specific case, such as supporting manufacturer endorsements or factory stability approvals.

EPCG Installation Certificate Submission Timeline & DGFT Amendments

Under the statutory amendment introduced through DGFT Public Notice No. 15/2024-25 dated 25 July 2024, the time limit for submitting the installation certificate under paragraph 5.04(a) of the Handbook of Procedures 2023 was significantly updated.

Key Timeline Provisions:

  • Three-Year General Deadline: The installation certificate is generally required to be submitted within three years from the date of completion of import.
  • Extension Option: The Regional Authority (RA) may allow an extension up to the validity of the export-obligation period, subject to payment of a composition fee of ₹10,000 per year.
  • Issuing Authority: The certificate may be obtained from the jurisdictional Customs authority or an independent Chartered Engineer, as provided under the applicable procedure.

Authorisation holders should check the latest DGFT provisions and their specific authorisation conditions before planning submission.

Common Challenges in EPCG Installation Certification

Businesses may encounter documentation or inspection-related issues during the certification process. Some common challenges include:

1. Mismatch in Machinery Details

Differences between the EPCG authorisation, Bill of Entry, invoice, and actual machinery identification details (such as model nomenclature or serial numbers) require professional engineering clarification before submission.

2. Incomplete Installation Records

Missing installation photographs, clear nameplate photos, technical specifications, or supporting documents can cause delays during DGFT portal verification.

3. Machinery Installed at a Different Location

If capital goods are installed or shifted to another unit or supporting manufacturer, the applicable EPCG conditions and fresh installation certificate requirements must be confirmed.

4. Delayed Certificate Submission

Businesses should monitor the applicable submission timeline and any extension requirements to avoid penalties, composition fees, or show-cause notices.

5. Incomplete Inspection Information

The on-site inspection should adequately identify the machinery and document the installation status based on verifiable physical evidence.
