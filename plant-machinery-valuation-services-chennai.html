What Is Plant and Machinery Valuation?

Plant and machinery valuation is the process of estimating the value of industrial equipment, production machinery, manufacturing systems, and related assets for a specified purpose and valuation date.

The assessment may involve reviewing technical specifications, inspecting the equipment, examining maintenance records, analysing depreciation, and considering relevant market information.

Depending on the assignment, the valuation may cover:

  • Manufacturing machinery and production equipment
  • CNC machines and industrial machine tools
  • Presses, lathes and forming machines
  • Packaging and processing equipment
  • Material handling equipment
  • Power generation and utility equipment
  • Factory production lines
  • Industrial plant and equipment installations

The valuation basis and approach should be selected according to the purpose of the assignment and the available evidence.

Why Do Businesses Need Plant and Machinery Valuation?

Businesses may require professional plant and machinery valuation for several commercial, financial, and operational purposes:

1. Buying and Selling Industrial Equipment

Before purchasing or selling used machinery, businesses need a reasonable understanding of its current value. A professional assessment can help evaluate the equipment's condition, specifications, and market position.

2. Insurance Purposes

Machinery valuation may support insurance-related requirements by providing information about the assets being insured. The required value basis should be confirmed with the insurer and relevant policy terms.

3. Financial Reporting and Asset Management

Companies may require asset valuation information for financial reporting, internal asset registers, and management decisions. The applicable accounting requirements and valuation basis should be confirmed for the assignment.

4. Business Acquisition and Mergers

During business acquisitions, restructuring, or mergers, plant and machinery may form a significant part of the company's asset base. Valuation helps stakeholders assess the equipment as part of the overall transaction.

5. Bank Finance and Loan Documentation

Financial institutions may request machinery valuation reports as part of certain lending or secured-finance processes. The required format, valuer eligibility, and scope depend on the institution's requirements.

6. Import and Customs-Related Requirements

Businesses importing used machinery may need technical inspection and valuation-related documentation for applicable import and customs procedures. Customs valuation must follow the relevant legal framework and should not be confused with a general market valuation.

Methods Used for Plant and Machinery Valuation

The appropriate valuation method depends on the asset type, intended purpose, available records, and market evidence.

1. Market Approach

The market approach considers information from comparable machinery sales and market transactions. The valuer may examine:

  • Similar machinery available in the market
  • Recent transaction information, where available
  • Manufacturer and model
  • Age and physical condition
  • Technical specifications
  • Location and market demand

Adjustments may be necessary when comparable equipment differs in condition, capacity, or configuration.

2. Cost Approach

The cost approach considers the cost of obtaining an equivalent or comparable asset, with appropriate adjustments for depreciation and obsolescence. The assessment may consider:

  • Replacement or reproduction cost
  • Physical deterioration
  • Functional obsolescence
  • Economic obsolescence
  • Remaining useful life

3. Income Approach

The income approach considers the future economic benefits attributable to an asset, where reliable information is available and the method is appropriate. It may be relevant for specialised machinery or equipment that contributes directly to measurable income generation.

The selected approach and any assumptions should be explained in the valuation report.

Factors That Affect Plant and Machinery Value

Several factors influence the current value of industrial machinery:

  • Age and Year of Manufacture: The manufacturing year and commissioning date provide useful information about the equipment's age. However, age alone does not determine its value.
  • Physical Condition: The inspection may identify wear and tear, corrosion, damaged parts, leakage, and other visible defects that affect the equipment's condition.
  • Operating Hours and Usage: Operating hours, production cycles, and usage intensity can help indicate the level of machinery utilisation.
  • Maintenance History: Service records, repairs, component replacements, and maintenance schedules provide evidence about how the equipment has been maintained.
  • Technical Specifications and Capacity: Manufacturer, model, rated capacity, production output, automation level, and technical configuration can influence marketability and value.
  • Depreciation and Obsolescence: Physical deterioration, functional limitations, and changes in technology may affect the value of machinery. Depreciation should be assessed appropriately rather than applying an arbitrary fixed percentage to every asset.
  • Spare Parts and Service Availability: Availability of replacement components and technical support can influence operating costs and future usability.
  • Market Demand: Demand for the machinery category, availability of comparable equipment, and current industry requirements may affect resale potential.

Plant and Machinery Valuation Process in Chennai

A typical valuation assignment involves the following structured stages:

Step 1: Understand the Valuation Requirement

The first step is to understand why the valuation is required, the type of assets involved, the intended users, and the required valuation basis.

Step 2: Collect Machinery Details

The client provides an asset list and available supporting documents, such as invoices, technical specifications, serial numbers, and maintenance records.

Step 3: Physical Inspection

A site inspection may be carried out at the factory or industrial premises to examine the machinery. The inspection may include:

  • Identification of machinery and equipment
  • Manufacturer and model verification
  • Serial number verification
  • Physical condition assessment
  • Installation and operating status
  • Photographic documentation
  • Review of available technical information

Step 4: Technical and Market Assessment

The available information and inspection findings are analysed. Relevant valuation approaches are selected based on the asset type, valuation purpose, and available market evidence.

Step 5: Valuation Report Preparation

The valuation report records the assets assessed, valuation basis, methodology, inspection details, assumptions, limitations, and conclusions.

Step 6: Report Delivery

The completed report is provided to the client in the agreed format and scope.
