Introduction

Businesses involved in manufacturing and exports often require raw materials, components and other inputs to produce goods for international markets. The Advance Authorisation Scheme under India's Foreign Trade Policy provides eligible exporters with a facility to import specified inputs without payment of basic customs duty, subject to applicable conditions and export obligations.

For certain applications, particularly those under the Self-Ratification Scheme, technical certification from a Chartered Engineer is an important part of the documentation process.

A Chartered Engineer Certificate helps establish the technical relationship between the proposed inputs and the export product. It provides information about raw material specifications, input quantities, manufacturing processes and technical requirements.

What Is Advance Authorisation?

Advance Authorisation is an export promotion scheme under India's Foreign Trade Policy. It allows eligible exporters to import inputs required for manufacturing export products without payment of basic customs duty, subject to the scheme's conditions.

The inputs must have the required technical and functional relationship with the export product and comply with the applicable input-output norms.

Depending on the application route, the authorisation may involve:

  • Standard Input Output Norms (SION): Pre-determined quantitative consumption standards notified by DGFT.
  • Ad-hoc norms: Custom norms fixed on an application-by-application basis by the Norms Committee.
  • Self-Ratification Scheme: An accelerated route where eligible exporters self-certify input requirements supported by a Chartered Engineer Certificate.
  • Other applicable provisions: Specific provisions and conditions under the Foreign Trade Policy and Handbook of Procedures.

The exporter must meet the relevant eligibility conditions, documentation requirements and export obligation requirements.

What Is a Chartered Engineer Certificate for Advance Authorisation?

A Chartered Engineer Certificate is a technical document issued by a suitably qualified Chartered Engineer for the relevant purpose under the applicable DGFT procedure.

Under the Self-Ratification Scheme, the Chartered Engineer examines the technical details of the proposed inputs and export products.

The assessment may include:

  • Description of the export product: Commercial trade name, grade, technical specifications, and end-use application.
  • Technical specifications of the export product: Chemical composition, physical parameters, dimensions, and performance criteria.
  • Raw material requirements: Identification of primary inputs, chemical additives, catalysts, or components.
  • Input descriptions and specifications: Detailed technical data sheets, international standards, and purity levels.
  • Quantity of each input required: Unit consumption calculations per unit of finished export product.
  • Manufacturing process: Process flow charts, reaction mechanisms, fabrication sequences, or assembly stages.
  • Technical drawings and designs: Engineering drawings, bill of materials (BOM), and assembly schematics where relevant.
  • Input consumption and wastage considerations: Unrecoverable process losses, recoverable scrap, by-products, and solvent recovery rates.

The certificate provides technical support for the applicant's declared input requirements. The Chartered Engineer must act within their relevant domain of competence.

When Is a Chartered Engineer Certificate Required?

The requirement depends on the Advance Authorisation application route and the applicable DGFT provisions.

1. Self-Ratification Scheme

Under the Self-Ratification Scheme, eligible exporters can apply where the export product does not have applicable SION or valid ad-hoc norms, or where additional inputs are proposed under the relevant scheme provisions. The application requires the prescribed Chartered Engineer Certificate in Appendix 4K, along with the relevant technical details.

2. Input Assessment Applications

A Chartered Engineer's technical examination may be relevant where the application requires verification of raw material specifications, input quantities, manufacturing processes and technical consumption requirements. The exact certificate format depends on the applicable DGFT provision.

3. Other Advance Authorisation Applications: Not every Advance Authorisation application automatically requires an Appendix 4K certificate. The requirement depends on the application category, applicable norms and current DGFT procedure. Exporters should verify the applicable documentation before arranging certification.

What Is Appendix 4K?

Appendix 4K is the prescribed format for a Chartered Engineer Certificate for fixation of SION or ad-hoc norms under the Self-Ratification Scheme, as specified in the DGFT Handbook of Procedures.

The certificate is furnished on the Chartered Engineer's official letterhead and submitted electronically or physically as mandated by DGFT.

According to the official format, it includes:

  • Chartered Engineer registration number and professional institute affiliation.
  • Date of registration and validity status.
  • Relevant technical domain or engineering branch competence (e.g., Mechanical, Chemical, Metallurgy).
  • Applicant and supporting manufacturer details (Name, IEC, Pan).
  • Factory address where the manufacturing process takes place.
  • Export product details, nomenclature, and commercial trade names.
  • Technical characteristics, dimensions, and grade specifications.
  • ITC (HS) classification of both finished export goods and imported inputs.
  • Export product quantity and proposed input quantities with precise units of measurement (UOM).
  • Chartered Engineer's physical/digital signature, official seal, and date.

The applicant also provides a declaration confirming the correctness of the information submitted. The complete Appendix 4E details and relevant technical information must accompany the certificate.

Important Technical Details Examined by a Chartered Engineer

A proper technical examination helps establish whether the proposed inputs are relevant to the manufacturing process and export product.

1. Export Product Details

The assessment begins with the comprehensive identification of the product proposed for export. Relevant information includes:

  • Product name and commercial trade description
  • Technical characteristics, chemistry, and performance limits
  • Material composition and metallurgical analysis
  • Product specifications, tolerance, and drawing references
  • ITC (HS) 8-digit tariff classification code
  • Export quantity and unit of measurement

2. Raw Material Specifications

Each proposed input should be clearly identified to eliminate generic or vague descriptions. The technical review considers:

  • Raw material chemical or technical name
  • Grade, purity level, and quality standards (e.g., ASTM, DIN, IS)
  • Chemical or physical properties and technical data sheets
  • Dimensions, gauge, thickness, and specifications
  • Harmonized System ITC (HS) classification
  • Proposed duty-free import quantity

3. Manufacturing Process

The manufacturing process helps explain how the proposed inputs are converted into the export product. Relevant details include:

  • Sequential manufacturing stages and production flow charts
  • Production methods (e.g., machining, forging, compounding, synthesis)
  • Processing parameters (temperature, pressure, duration)
  • Material consumption per batch or run
  • Process losses (irrecoverable dust, oxidation, vapor)
  • Wastage and by-products generated

4. Input Consumption Requirements

The technical assessment rigorously examines the quantity of each input required for manufacturing the proposed export product. The consumption requirements must be supported by appropriate engineering calculations, stoichiometry, bill of materials (BOM), and pilot or historical production logs.

5. Wastage and By-Products

Where relevant, the assessment details process wastage, recoverable scrap, and marketable by-products generated during manufacturing. The prescribed Appendix 4K format contains specific clauses relating to industrial solvents, recovery percentages, recoverable wastage values, and by-product accountability.

Documents Required for Chartered Engineer Certification

Exporters should prepare the relevant technical and application documents before requesting certification. Commonly required information includes:

Company Credentials

Import Export Code (IEC) certificate, GST registration, factory license, and supporting manufacturer agreement where applicable.

Product Specifications

Detailed export product description, chemical composition, mechanical properties, and relevant international quality standards.

Input Master List

Proposed duty-free input list with technical specifications, grades, ITC (HS) codes, and unit consumption calculations.

Manufacturing Flow Chart

Comprehensive step-by-step production flow chart, input entry points, process loss stages, and scrap recovery mechanisms.

Engineering Drawings

Approved fabrication drawings, technical blueprints, assembly drawings, or chemical formulation sheets where applicable.

Appendix 4E & Orders

Draft Appendix 4E, confirmed export purchase orders, or past Advance Authorisations/norms if available.

Note: Additional documents may be required depending on the export product, manufacturing complexity, and specific DGFT application requirements.

Chartered Engineer Certification Process for Advance Authorisation

The engineering certification process generally follows six systematic stages, subject to applicable DGFT procedures:

Step 1: Initial Requirement Review

The applicant's Advance Authorisation requirement, export product classification, and proposed DGFT application route (SION, Ad-hoc, or Self-Ratification) are reviewed to establish the applicable certification scope.

Step 2: Document Collection & Verification

Relevant company, manufacturing facility, raw material, and product technical specifications are gathered and verified against statutory business registries.

Step 3: Technical Document Examination

The Chartered Engineer reviews product drawings, chemical formulations, bill of materials, manufacturing process steps, and factory machinery capabilities.

Step 4: Input Requirement & Wastage Assessment

The proposed input quantities, net consumption norms, wastage factors, and technical relationship to the export product are rigorously evaluated and calculated.

Step 5: Chartered Engineer Certification (Appendix 4K)

Where the assessment supports the exporter's declaration, the Chartered Engineer issues the official certificate in the prescribed DGFT format (such as Appendix 4K) under official seal and signature.

Step 6: DGFT Portal Submission & RA Approval

The exporter uploads the signed certificate and Appendix 4E on the DGFT online portal. The concerned Regional Authority (RA) or Norms Committee examines the application for final authorization grant.

Common Mistakes to Avoid in Advance Authorisation Certification

Incomplete or inconsistent technical information can create difficulties during application examination or lead to queries and delays by DGFT Regional Authorities. Exporters should pay close attention to the following pitfalls:

  • 1. Incorrect Input Specifications: Input descriptions should accurately reflect the material's technical characteristics, grade, purity, and specifications rather than trade nicknames.
  • 2. Incorrect Input Quantities & Disproportionate Wastage: Proposed quantities must be substantiated by sound engineering calculations, production trials, or chemical stoichiometry rather than arbitrary round figures.
  • 3. Incomplete Manufacturing Details: The manufacturing process description must clearly delineate how each imported input is utilized in the finished product.
  • 4. Missing Technical Documents: Crucial product specifications, engineering blueprints, process flowcharts, and Appendix 4E details must be prepared and attached comprehensively.
  • 5. Incorrect ITC (HS) Classification: Mismatched 8-digit ITC (HS) codes between the application, commercial invoices, and input list cause severe customs clearance blocks.
  • 6. Using an Incorrect Certificate Format: Exporters should verify the exact certificate format applicable to their specific Advance Authorisation route rather than assuming Appendix 4K applies across all categories.
